§ 01 Introduction

What is IKS?

Indian Knowledge Systems — often abbreviated IKS — is a collective term for the interconnected bodies of knowledge developed, preserved and transmitted in the Indian subcontinent across many generations.

These traditions were carried through texts, oral transmission and practice: in mathematics and astronomy, in medicine and the life of the body, in grammar and philosophy, in the arts, in architecture, agriculture and the organisation of society.

Studying them today means asking a very modern set of questions — how was knowledge created, organised, taught, tested and applied? — and how those questions connect with the disciplines of the contemporary world.

Understanding the knowledge tradition

Three ideas recur across these traditions. They are not dogmas but habits of mind — a way of holding knowledge so that it survives, adapts and stays useful.

Textual TransmissionOral TransmissionPractical Transmission
परम्परा I — Continuity of knowledge

Paramparā

Transmission across generations

Knowledge in these traditions was carried through structured transmission — teacher to student, text to commentary, practice to practice. This chain of continuity kept traditions alive, self-aware and able to adapt across many centuries.

दृष्टि II — Perspective & worldview

Dṛṣṭi

Ways of understanding

Knowledge traditions involve distinctive ways of seeing — assumptions about reality, society and human experience that shape how questions are asked. Understanding a tradition's dṛṣṭi means understanding its lens, not only its conclusions.

लौकिकम् III — Practical relevance

Laukika-Prayojana

Knowledge and practical life

Much of this knowledge was intimately practical — measuring land, keeping calendars, treating illness, governing, building, cultivating. Theory and application grew together, a relationship that modern fields continue to examine.

§ 06 Relevance

IKS in the Contemporary World

“Tradition is not only something to remember.
It is something to study.”

Across universities and research institutes, the knowledge traditions of India are being examined with the tools of modern scholarship — critically, comparatively, and source-first. These are active areas of study today.
